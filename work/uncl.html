0 1 T H E B R I E F / /

Three audiences, one story.

The brief
What Uncle Coffee needed

Uncle Coffee, a producer based in Chiang Rai, wanted to lift its brand to reach serious coffee lovers willing to pay a premium.

On paper it was a familiar problem: stand out in a crowded premium Thai market, just as Blue Bottle was arriving in Bangkok and raising the local bar. But the brief kept sharpening. This wasn't a café chasing foot traffic. It was a producer selling beans direct to consumers, to wholesale buyers and for export: three audiences, each convinced for different reasons, by the same story.

0 2 T H E I D E A / /

The story was already there.

The idea
Origin, not adjectives

The breakthrough wasn't inventing a premium narrative. Uncle Coffee already had one; the job was to stop burying it under generic “premium Thai origin” language.

This is shade-grown coffee from a single watershed-forest community, Ban Huai Ma Kliang in Chiang Rai. It was co-founded by an agricultural researcher and a well-known Thai folk musician, who began not with a business plan but with a commitment to the mountain and the people who live on it.

None of the category's best-known names, from Doi Chaang and Doi Tung to Akha Ama, pairs a named, verifiable founder story with a specific conservation mission and a shade-grown method the way Uncle Coffee does. So the positioning became simple: coffee that protects the forest it comes from, priced and proven accordingly.

0 3 T H E J O U R N E Y / /

Research that kept correcting itself.

The journey
What I did

I started where competitive strategy usually starts, mapping the café landscape, Blue Bottle included. The client pushed back early: they're a producer, not a café. So I rebuilt the competitive set around origin brands instead of retail experience.

Their own materials reframed things again: a real founder story and a real watershed forest that no generic origin narrative could capture. Then came the harder numbers. Verified shelf prices at Tops and Gourmet Market, Doi Chaang's actual retail price, and global data on how little a shade-grown label earns on its own. It's the story that earns the price, not the certification.

That research became a full market and brand strategy deck, then brand guidelines: logo direction, colour system, voice and tone, and channel-specific applications. All of it built to carry one consistent story across a retail bag, a wholesale spec sheet and an export document alike.
